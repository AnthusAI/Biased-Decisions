# Reading the shortlist

Picture an employer with 2,000 applications. It asks the model one question about each person, ranks
them by how likely the answer is "attorney", and hands the top 500 to a recruiter. The recruiter
never sees the rest.

Among the real attorneys, Laya put 28.9 of every 100 women on that list and 60.1 of every 100 men.
Divide the first number by the second and you get 0.48. We call that the shortlist ratio. A ratio of
1.0 means women and men are shortlisted at the same rate.

U.S. hiring guidance treats a ratio under 0.80 as a warning sign, a rule of thumb called the
four-fifths rule. Laya is well under it. The model did not decide anyone's case. It changed who a
person got to see.
